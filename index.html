The essentials

A happy cat starts at home

A safe, enriching environment supports your cat’s wellbeing. Build a gentle routine around clean water, a tidy litter tray, daily play, grooming and a quiet place to rest.

  • Keep litter trays clean and accessible.
  • Provide scratching posts and safe climbing spaces.
  • Make time for interactive play every day.
  • Arrange routine veterinary check-ups.
Food for thought

Nutrition, without the confusion

Age, activity and health affect what your cat needs. These are starting points — your vet can help with individual needs.

💧

Fresh water

Keep fresh water available in quiet, accessible spots.

🍽️

Complete food

Choose nutritionally complete food suited to your cat’s life stage.

⚖️

Healthy portions

Measure meals and monitor body condition. Ask your vet about weight changes.

Avoid sudden diet changes. Never give human medication to your cat unless a veterinarian specifically instructs you to.

Know the signs

Your cat’s health matters

Changes in normal habits can be important. If something seems unusual, contact a veterinary professional.

Notice changes early

Watch for changes in appetite, thirst, litter-box habits, movement, grooming or behaviour. Keep notes to share with your vet.

🚨 Urgent warning signs

Breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, major injury or straining to urinate require urgent veterinary attention.

This website provides general educational information, not veterinary diagnosis or treatment. Always consult a veterinarian about your cat’s health.

Meet the kitties

Cats come in all personalities

Breed can offer clues, but every cat is an individual. Temperament also depends on socialisation and life experience.

Domestic Shorthair

A varied group of companion cats with many coat colours and personalities.

Tabby

Tabby describes a coat pattern, not a breed. Many cats have beautiful striped or swirled markings.

British Shorthair

Known for a plush coat and rounded appearance; care needs vary by individual.

A forever friend

Ready to welcome a cat?

Adoption is a long-term commitment. Think about time, costs, home safety and the cat’s individual needs. Ask the shelter about health history and temperament.
